Scaling an AI Forecasting Agent on Azure

How a right-sized Azure data platform took an AI forecasting agent from a promising pilot to a workload the business runs on every day.

01 / The problem

A pilot that couldn't survive contact with real data

A forecasting AI agent performed well in a pilot built on a sampled, laptop-sized dataset. Connected to the real enterprise data — spread across on-prem systems and several SaaS applications — it became slow, expensive to run, and unpredictable, because the underlying platform was never designed to serve AI workloads at that scale.

Client perspective

"The model was never the issue. We were asking a platform built for monthly reports to serve a forecasting agent in near real time, on ten times the data. It just wasn't built for that job."— Head of Data Engineering, cloud modernization client
02 / The approach

A platform built for the workload, not the pilot

→

Baseline

We inventoried existing cloud services, data volumes, cost, and availability requirements.

Architect

We selected the right mix of Azure data services and resilience patterns for the forecasting workload.

Migrate

We moved and validated pipelines and datasets with a controlled, rollback-ready cutover.

Optimize

We tuned for cost per workload, query latency, and the ability to scale out when the agent's demand spikes.

03 / The results

A platform that scales with the agent, not against it

Results

Rebuilt on Microsoft Fabric and Synapse with resilient ingestion patterns, the platform now serves 14x the data volume of the original pilot at 35% lower cost per workload, with 99.95% uptime since cutover and the ability to scale out for a demand spike in under four minutes.

Business and AI impact

The forecasting agent now runs on the business's full data, on a predictable budget, without the platform becoming the next thing standing in its way.
