Making AI Agents Safe to Deploy

How data classification and least-privilege access let a company deploy AI agents across support, finance, and HR without losing track of what those agents could see.

01 / The problem

Nobody could say what the agents could see

As the company rolled out AI agents across support, finance, and HR, security couldn't confidently answer a basic question: what data can this agent actually see? Access had grown broad and informal over years of ad-hoc requests, there was no consistent classification of sensitive fields, and agent queries weren't logged any differently from routine application traffic.

Client perspective

"Before this, if you asked me what an agent could see, the honest answer was 'probably more than it should.' Now I can tell you in an afternoon, with the audit trail to prove it."— CISO, enterprise client
02 / The approach

Classify, restrict, and audit every agent's access

Sensitive data sourcesBroad, informal access

Classify

We tagged sensitive fields and datasets so "what this agent can see" has a concrete, documented answer.

Restrict

We defined a least-privilege role per agent instead of reusing broad, shared service accounts.

Audit

We logged every agent query distinctly, with lineage back to the source, so access is reviewable after the fact.

Review

We put a recertification and pre-launch review gate in front of every new or changed agent.

03 / The results

An answer in an afternoon, not a guess

Results

Classifying sensitive fields and moving every agent to a least-privilege role removed 63% of over-permissioned access during rollout. Audit logging now covers 100% of AI agent data access, and the pre-launch review gate has caught seven policy risks before they ever reached production.

Business and AI impact

Least-privilege, audited access didn't slow AI adoption down — it's what let the business say yes to the next agent with confidence instead of hoping nothing would go wrong.
